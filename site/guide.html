This guide walks through what prev does, in the order you are likely to meet it. Keyboard shortcuts are listed in the README; on macOS, use ⌘ where this guide says Ctrl and ⌥ (Option) where it says Alt, and the menu bar lists each shortcut. Shortcuts can be changed; see Settings.

Documents shown: a sample form, a sample annual report, a sample account statement, a newsletter and a landscape image, all fictional and made for prev.

Opening files

Open files with Ctrl+O, from the file manager's "Open With", from the command line (prev report.pdf photo.jpg), or by dropping them on a prev window (see Drag and drop for drops that do more than open files). Each document gets its own window; images opened together share one window with a thumbnail strip. prev keeps a single running instance, so opening another file from the launcher adds a window instead of starting over. On macOS, files opened from Finder or dropped on prev's icon in the Dock go to the running prev the same way.

Reading PDFs

The toolbar holds the page number, zoom, fit to page or width, actual size, and the view mode: continuous scroll, single page or two pages side by side. Image windows share its layout: what is shown and the view on the left, editing, the panels and Export on the right. Ctrl+scroll zooms in and out around the pointer, and pages stay sharp at any zoom because they render in tiles on background threads. Ctrl+drag pans around a zoomed-in view whatever tool is chosen, and in image windows a plain drag on the picture does too (unless the selection tool or markup is in use). The middle mouse button scrolls as in a browser.

The sidebar (the button at the far left, or Ctrl+Alt+2 to 5) has four tabs: page thumbnails, the table of contents, highlights and notes, and bookmarks (Ctrl+D bookmarks the current page). Drag the sidebar's edge to make it wider; the thumbnails grow with it.

In two-page mode pages sit in pairs, 1 and 2, 3 and 4, and the arrow keys move a pair at a time.

Search (Ctrl+F) highlights every match and counts them as it goes; Ctrl+G and Ctrl+Shift+G step through them. Text can be selected and copied across pages; double-click selects a word and triple-click a line. Links inside the document and to the web work, and Ctrl+Shift+F starts a full-screen slideshow. F11 makes the window full screen; on macOS the View menu's Enter Full Screen does too. The inspector (Ctrl+I) shows the file and the document's details.

Print (Ctrl+P) opens the system's print dialog: the desktop's, through its portal, on Linux; Windows' print dialog; and on macOS the print panel, whose preview shows the pages as prev draws them.

Marking up

The markup toolbar (the pen button on the right, or Ctrl+Shift+A) follows Preview's:

  • Select moves, resizes and restyles annotations; Delete removes the selected one.
  • Rectangular selection chooses an area to copy as an image, or to crop the page to (Ctrl+K).
  • Sketch turns strokes that look like a rectangle, oval or line into that shape; Draw keeps them as drawn.
  • Shapes: rectangle, rounded rectangle, oval, line, arrow, star, polygon, speech bubble, a loupe that magnifies the page under it, and a mask that dims everything but a hole.
  • Text box and Note, with fonts, sizes, colors and alignment. While you type in a text box, a bar above it makes the selected text bold (Ctrl+B), italic (Ctrl+I) or another color.
  • Highlight, underline and strikethrough over selected text, in five colors.
  • Sign and Redact, described below.
  • Border color, fill color, line width and dashes for shapes.

Paste (Ctrl+V) puts an image or text from the clipboard on the current page, whatever tool is chosen: an image at its own size (smaller if it doesn't fit), text as a text box. An image file copied in the file manager (or Finder) pastes as the image; on Linux, pasting images needs wl-clipboard. Pages copied in prev paste as pages instead, after the selected one, when they are the latest thing copied.

Everything is saved as standard PDF annotations that other viewers show and edit. Undo and redo (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Shift+Z) cover every change, markup and page edits alike. Their buttons are on the toolbar, and move to the markup bar while it is open.

Forms

Click into text fields and type; checkboxes, radio buttons and drop-down menus work as they do in other viewers. Values are saved into the form so other apps read them.

Signatures

The Sign menu keeps as many signatures as you like. Click one to place it on the current page, then drag and resize it like any annotation.

Create Signature offers three ways in:

  • Draw with the mouse, a pen or the touchpad. Choose the ink color and the pen's thickness under the pad; changing them redraws what you signed.
  • Type your name, shown in a handwriting font in the ink you choose.
  • Image takes a photo or scan of a signature on paper and makes the paper transparent.

Editing pages

In the page thumbnails, click a page to select it, Ctrl+click to add pages and Shift+click to select a range. Then:

  • Drag the selection to reorder; a line shows where it will go.
  • Rotate with the toolbar buttons (Ctrl+L, Ctrl+R).
  • Delete with the Delete key.
  • Copy with Ctrl+C and paste with Ctrl+V after the selected page, in the same window or another document's. Pasted pages keep their annotations and form fields.
  • Drop PDF files on the thumbnails to insert their pages where you let go.
  • Drag thumbnails out of the window to copy the pages into another prev window, where they go where you drop them, or into the file manager as a PDF. Hold Shift while dropping to move them instead. See Drag and drop.

The Pages menu (the stacked-pages button, beside the rotate buttons) has Insert Blank Page, Insert from File, Copy and Paste, Crop to Selection, Select All Pages and Delete. Every page edit can be undone.

Redacting

The Redact tool marks what should go: drag a box over it, or select text first and then pick the tool. Marks are ordinary annotations until you press Apply and confirm. Applying removes the text, image pixels and drawings under each mark from the file and draws black boxes in their place. The next save rewrites the whole file, so no earlier revision keeps the content, and prev deletes the earlier versions of the file it kept. This cannot be undone.

Exporting

Export (the export button, a page with an arrow near the right of the toolbar, or Ctrl+Shift+S) writes a new file:

  • PDF, optionally:
  • Reduce file size: images are downsampled to 150 dpi and saved as JPEG.
  • Flatten annotations and form fields: markup and filled-in fields are drawn into the pages and can no longer be edited. Redaction marks not yet applied are left out.
  • Encrypt with a password (AES-256): the password is needed to open the file.
  • PNG, JPEG, TIFF, WebP or OpenEXR at 72 to 600 dpi, with a quality choice for JPEG. TIFF puts every page in one file; the others save a file per page, numbered after the name you choose.

Tick "Only the selected pages" to export just the pages chosen in the sidebar.

Images

prev opens PNG, JPEG, GIF (animated too), WebP, AVIF, HEIC, TIFF, BMP, ICO, TGA, PNM, QOI, JPEG 2000, OpenEXR, Radiance HDR and camera RAW. RAW photos open looking like the JPEG the camera would have made: prev compares the RAW with the small JPEG preview the camera stores in the file and gives it the same brightness, contrast and color balance. The toolbar rotates, flips, crops and resizes (Adjust Size). Adjust Color (Ctrl+Shift+C) has exposure, contrast, saturation, temperature, tint, sepia, sharpness and levels. Undo and redo are on the toolbar. The inspector (Ctrl+I) shows the file and camera details and can remove location info and edit keywords and a description. Export asks for the format (PNG, JPEG, WebP, TIFF, BMP, TGA, QOI, PPM or OpenEXR) and, for JPEG, the quality, then where to save; the choices are kept for the next export from the window. Drop more image files on the window to add them to its thumbnail sidebar.

Images take markup too: the Markup button (Ctrl+Shift+A) brings up the same markup bar as PDFs, with Sketch, Draw, shapes, text boxes, notes and signatures. Markup stays while the window is open and can be moved, restyled and undone; rotating, cropping and color changes wait until it is gone. Paste works on images too, and starts the markup if there is none yet. Export draws it into the pixels of the copy you save, at the image's full resolution. Closing the window, or quitting, with markup you haven't exported asks first, with a button to export.

SVG and Markdown

SVG drawings open sharp at any zoom, in the image window. They can't be edited, so the tools that change pixels are greyed out, but Export saves the drawing as a picture in any of the image formats, at its actual size or two or four times larger (Size in the Export dialog, which shows the picture's size in pixels).

Markdown files show with tables, task lists, syntax highlighted code and images, and reload when the file changes on disk. The toolbar makes the text larger or smaller (Ctrl+= and Ctrl+-, Ctrl+0 for the normal size), searches the document (Ctrl+F, then Enter or the arrows for the next and previous match) and opens the inspector with the file's details and word count. Code is colored to suit the light or dark look, and pictures show at their own size, shrunk only to fit the page. Export saves the whole document as one picture, in any of the image formats, at its actual size or twice as sharp; very long documents are scaled down to fit the 16,383 pixel height some formats allow.

Inspector

The inspector (the info button on the toolbar, or Ctrl+I) opens a panel on the right with what prev knows about the file. Close it with its close button or Ctrl+I again. Every inspector starts with the file itself: its name, the folder it is in, its size and when it was last changed.

  • PDFs add the document's own details, where the file has them: title, author, subject, keywords, when it was created and last changed, the application that made it and the PDF producer, the PDF version and whether it is encrypted, and with what. Under Pages come the number of pages and the current page's size, in millimeters and inches.
  • Images add the format and size in pixels, then what the camera recorded: make, model and lens, exposure time, f-number, ISO and focal length, and when the photo was taken. Where the photo says where it was taken, Location shows the coordinates, and Remove Location Info takes them out of the file. Keywords and a description can be added and saved into JPEG, PNG and WebP files. Revert To lists the earlier versions prev kept, with their dates and sizes, and puts one back.
  • Markdown adds the number of words.

Drag and drop

Saturn: NASA/JPL/Space Science Institute, public domain, shown on Wikimedia Commons. The newsletter is a sample made for prev.

Drag and drop works both ways: things dragged into prev land where you let go, and pages, text, areas and images drag out of prev to other prev windows, the file manager and other apps. While a drag that prev can take is over a window, the window is highlighted; over the page thumbnails, a line shows where the pages would go.

Things dropped on the page view land on the page; things dropped on the sidebar (a document's page thumbnails, an image window's images) go there. Hold Ctrl (Control on macOS, not ⌘) while dropping on the page view to send the drop to the sidebar instead, without aiming for it.

Whatever tool is chosen, dropped images and text become annotations the select tool can move, and Undo takes them back. An image annotation dragged from a page onto the thumbnails becomes a new page there too: it is copied, and stays on its page unless you hold Shift (or Command on macOS), which moves it.

Dragging out of prev

What How to drag it What it carries
Selected text With the Select tool, press on the selected text and drag The text, for any app that takes text
An area With the rectangular selection tool, choose an area, then press inside it and drag A PNG image of the area at twice the screen's resolution
Pages Drag thumbnails out of the window (inside the window, dragging reorders them) The pages: another prev window inserts them; the file manager saves them as a PDF named after the document and pages, such as Report (pages 2–4).pdf
An image in an image window Drag it out of the thumbnail sidebar The file itself, which the file manager copies and other apps open

On Linux and macOS, a small picture of the pages, area or image follows the pointer while it is dragged; Windows shows its own drag cursor. Dragged images and text are copied. Pages are copied unless you hold Shift (or Command on macOS) when you let go, which moves them: they are removed from the document they came from. A page dragged out and dropped back on the same window moves there with Shift, or is copied there without it. An image dragged out of the sidebar and dropped back on its own window is left alone.

For pages going to the file manager, prev writes the PDF to a drag folder in its cache (~/.cache/prev, ~/Library/Caches/prev on macOS, or %LOCALAPPDATA%\prev\cache on Windows); it is removed when the next drag starts and when prev quits.

Browsers and other apps

  • Chromium hands over the picture itself, so it arrives at full size. Browsers that give only the picture's web address have it downloaded (with curl, within 20 seconds and 64 MB); on Linux without curl installed, the address arrives as text instead. Windows includes curl.
  • Links to anything other than a picture or a PDF arrive as their address, as text; prev does not fetch them.
  • On Linux, drag and drop does not need wl-clipboard; copying and pasting images does.
  • On Windows and macOS, these keys work for drags from any app.
  • Wayland tells the window under a drag nothing about the keys held. prev sees Shift and Ctrl during drags from other apps because Hyprland moves the keyboard focus with the pointer; under compositors that do not, they work only for drags that start in prev. Hyprland also never reports whether a drop copied or moved, so prev goes by Shift at both ends.

Not yet

  • Annotations cannot be dragged out of a window, nor from one document to another; copy an area instead. Image annotations can go to their own window's sidebar, as above.
  • Images dropped on an image window's sidebar join it after its images, not where they are let go.
  • An image window showing a single image has no sidebar to drag it from; drag it from the file manager instead.
  • The page thumbnails do not scroll by themselves while dragging near their top or bottom; the scroll wheel scrolls them during a drag.

The assistant

The assistant is a chat beside your document: ask about the file and it reads what it needs, shows you where things are, and works in the file for you with the same tools as an AI agent. The robot head on the toolbar, before the markup button, opens it on the right. Each window has its own chat, kept while the panel is closed; New Chat starts over, and Stop ends an answer under way. When a chat grows longer than a local model can read at once, its earlier part is summarized so the chat can go on, and the panel says so.

The model gets only what it asks for through the tools, never the whole file up front. With a local model nothing leaves your computer; with a cloud model, what it reads goes to the provider, so use one you trust with those files.

Adding a model

Open Settings, the Assistant tab, and pick a provider under Add a model:

  • Ollama, or another server on your computer that speaks OpenAI's API, such as LM Studio or llama.cpp's: prev finds the running server and lists its models, with whether each can use tools, which the assistant needs, and whether it sees pictures. For Ollama, Context sets how much the model reads at once: more lets it take in more of a file, but takes more memory and can answer more slowly. Use another address reaches a server on another computer. With no server running, prev says so and links to its download.
  • Anthropic, OpenAI or Google Gemini: a cloud model needs an API key from the provider; Get an API key opens the page that makes one. Paste it and Continue, and prev lists the provider's chat models, the recommended one first. The key is kept in the system's keychain (the Keychain on macOS, the Credential Manager on Windows, the Secret Service on Linux), never in prev's settings file, and once for all that provider's models; Change key replaces it. A new OpenAI or Anthropic account needs some credit bought before its key works.

Add sends the model one short message, and adds it once it answers; if it does not, the reason shows under it, with a link to the page that fixes it where there is one, such as billing. A model the list leaves out can be added by its name. The Assistant tab lists the models added, with the one in use; the panel's menu under the field switches model at any time, even mid-chat, grouped by provider.

Chatting

Type in the field and press Enter. The answer streams in as Markdown, with each tool the model uses as a line of its own, and a small picture for the tools that look at a page. Models that think before they answer show their reasoning faintly as it comes, folded under Thoughts once they answer. The assistant does what you ask it without asking again: the switches that make prev ask before an agent acts are for outside agents. A change it makes is one step of Undo, as yours are; signing and applying redactions cannot be undone, so it does them only when asked. A model that cannot see pictures gets the page's text instead.

Working with AI agents

An AI agent, such as Claude Code or a local model in an MCP client, can work in prev for you: read a document, show you something in it, mark it up, fill a form, sign, redact, rearrange pages, edit an image and export. prev runs an MCP server, prev --mcp, which the agent starts and talks to over standard input and output. It passes the agent's requests to the prev you are using, starting prev if it is not running, so you can watch the agent work in your own windows. Nothing listens on the network, and prev bundles no model: what the agent reads goes to the model the agent uses, so use one you trust with those files.

Setting up an agent

There is nothing to set up in prev. Add it to the agent as an MCP server that runs prev --mcp. For Claude Code:

claude mcp add prev -- prev --mcp

Where prev is not on the path, give the whole path: /Applications/prev.app/Contents/MacOS/prev on macOS, %LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\prev\prev.exe on Windows, and flatpak run run.prev.Prev for the Flatpak. For a local model, use an MCP client that works with it and add prev the same way; most take a file like this:

{ "mcpServers": { "prev": { "command": "prev", "args": ["--mcp"] } } }

The first time an agent connects, prev asks whether to let it control prev, in the window in front. Allow is remembered; Don't Allow lasts until prev quits. Settings lists the agents you allowed, with Forget to be asked again, and Allow outside control turns all of it off.

Asking before an agent acts

Settings has a switch for each kind of thing an agent can do: read a file, change the view or a window, mark up, edit, sign, apply redactions, and export. With a switch on, prev shows what the agent asks to do in the window it acts on, with Allow and Don't Allow, and the agent waits for your answer. Signing and applying redactions ask at first, as Undo cannot take them back; the others start off. The agent is told which tools ask, so it can tell you to look at prev. The switches are for outside agents; prev's own assistant acts on what you ask it without asking again.

What agents can do

  • Read: the windows open, a document's pages, text with its positions, search, annotations, form fields, saved signatures, pictures of pages and images, and Markdown files.
  • Show you: go to a page or a place on it, zoom, change the view mode, outline an area or some text for a few seconds, open and close the sidebar, inspector and other panels, and size, place, focus and close windows. On Wayland a window cannot place itself, so there only its size changes.
  • Mark up: highlights, notes, text boxes, shapes, arrows and redaction marks, on PDFs and on images.
  • Edit: rotate, delete, move, insert and crop pages, fill form fields, place pictures, and crop, rotate, resize and adjust the color of images. A model that changes an image, such as to remove its background, takes it from prev and hands back the changed one, and prev keeps the image as it was among its versions.
  • Sign, redact and export: place one of your saved signatures, apply redaction marks, and export to a new file.

Agents work as you do: each change saves by itself and is one step of Undo, so you can take back what an agent did.

Saving and versions

There is no Save command: edits to PDFs and images are written in place a moment after you stop, and when a window closes. Before the first write, prev keeps the original as a version in the version history folder (~/.local/share/prev/versions; %APPDATA%\prev\versions on Windows; ~/Library/Application Support/prev/versions on macOS), unless the settings say otherwise. For images, the inspector's Revert To list puts an earlier version back; Revert To for PDFs is still to come.

Settings

Settings opens over the current window, from the gear button at the end of the toolbar or Ctrl+,. Close it with its close button, Escape or a click outside it. Changes apply to every window at once. Its five tabs are General, with the languages and the default app; Appearance, with light or dark, colors and how windows look; Assistant, with the models for the assistant; Agents, for AI agents; and Storage, with where prev keeps your files:

  • Appearance: follow the system's light or dark setting, or choose one.
  • Language: follow the system's language (the default), or choose one of the languages prev has been translated into, each listed by its own name.
  • Input language: the language you type in, which sets the side an empty text field starts on: the right for Arabic, Hebrew, Persian, Urdu and the other right to left languages. By default it follows the keyboard layout in use (on Windows, the input language; on macOS, the input source in the menu bar), so switching layouts moves the cursor of an empty field. Text you type keeps its own direction whichever you choose.
  • Use the system accent color: colors are built from the system's accent color: the desktop theme's on Linux, and the one set in Windows' or macOS's settings. Off, or where the system has none, a row of colors below it picks the color instead; prev's blue is the first.
  • Hide the toolbar when the pointer leaves: see below.
  • Animations: off at first, so bars, panels and dialogs appear at once; on, they move into place. prev also stops moving things when the system asks for reduced motion.
  • Overlay transparency: lets the page show through the floating toolbar, 0 to 90%, 25% at first; its buttons stay solid.
  • Corner radius: rounds dialogs and the floating toolbar, from square corners at 0 to a full pill at 32; 20 at first.
  • Default app: Make Default makes prev the app that opens PDFs, images, SVG drawings and Markdown files. It changes the system's setting and saves nothing in prev's own. A dot beside the button shows how many of those file types open with prev now: red for none, amber for some, green for all, with the count below.
    • On Linux it sets prev for all 41 types in ~/.config/mimeapps.list. That needs prev's desktop entry, which the packages and scripts/install.sh install.
    • On macOS it asks for PDF, PNG, JPEG, HEIC, GIF, TIFF, WebP and AVIF, and macOS asks you to confirm each. For other types, such as camera RAW, use Get Info on a file in Finder, then Open with and Change All. prev must run from prev.app.
    • Windows lets only you choose default apps, so there the button opens prev's page in Settings, Apps, Default apps.
  • Assistant: the models the assistant can talk to, and adding one; see Adding a model.
  • Outside control: whether AI agents may control prev, the agents you allowed, and what prev asks you about before an agent acts; see Working with AI agents.
  • Storage: where prev keeps signatures, version history and bookmarks.

Keyboard shortcuts can be changed in the [keys] table of the settings file, prev.toml; the Settings dialog has no place for them. The README lists the actions and how to write a shortcut. Menus on macOS and the hints prev shows name the shortcuts in effect.

The floating toolbar

With "Hide the toolbar when the pointer leaves" on (or the top-panel button at the end of the toolbar), the toolbar floats over the document as a rounded bar, and the markup bar floats along the bottom. They slide in while the pointer is over the window and go away when it leaves, so the whole window shows the page. Sidebars and panels stay clear of them.

Where files go

Settings are kept in prev.toml, which lists every setting, including where prev keeps your files:

On Linux, for example:

signatures = "~/.local/share/prev/signatures"
versions = "~/.local/share/prev/versions"
bookmarks = "~/.local/share/prev/bookmarks.toml"

The Flatpak keeps its own under ~/.var/app/run.prev.Prev.

Change them in the Storage section: type a path (~ works) and press Enter or Apply, or pick a folder with Choose. prev checks that the folder exists and can be written, uses the new place at once and saves it to the file. Files already at the old place stay there; move them over to keep using them. The paths are filled in when the file is first made and then stay as they are.

Looks

prev follows the system's light or dark setting and reduced motion setting, and its accent color. It looks the same on Windows and macOS, in each system's own window; on the Mac its menus are in the menu bar, and its shortcuts use ⌘ instead of Ctrl. In narrow windows, toolbar groups that don't fit move into a More menu, which closes as soon as you choose something from it (a button that opens its own menu, such as Pages, keeps it open under that menu). The search field grows into whatever room the toolbar has left.

On Linux desktops that draw no title bar above a window, such as Hyprland, niri and river, the toolbar ends with a button that closes the window, and a window with no file open has one in its corner.
